Why it matters

Your child is already hearing who they are. The question is from whom.

The playground says“You’re not good enough.”
The screen says“You need to look like this.”
God says“I am fearfully and wonderfully made.”

Children decide who they are long before they can explain it. The words they hear, and the words they say about themselves, become the voice in their head on hard days.

Declare Kids puts God’s words in your child’s mouth, a little every day, in a way that feels like a game and not a lecture. Over weeks and months, “God made me on purpose” and “I can do hard things with His help” stop being sentences on a screen and become what your child believes.

Questions parents ask

Before you start.

My child can’t read well yet. Will this work?

Yes. Tap “Read it to me” and the declaration is read out loud, then your child repeats it while holding the button. In 5–7 mode the words are bigger and the hold is a little longer. Many families read it first and let their child echo.

I don’t want more screen time.

Neither do we. There are no videos, no ads and no feed. Three declarations take about five minutes, then the app celebrates and they’re done. It’s designed to be used together, like a modern family devotion card.

Is it safe for my child?

Yes. There’s no chat, no messaging and no ads. Your child can’t talk to anyone or be contacted in the app. Settings are behind a quick grown-ups check, and every declaration is gentle and age-appropriate.

I have more than one child.

One membership covers the whole family. Add up to six children, each with their own name, animal friend, age setting, stars, stickers and streak. Switching between them is one tap.

Is it biblical? Which translation do you use?

Every declaration is rooted in Scripture, and the verse is shown on every card with its reference. Verses are quoted from the New International Version (NIV). The declarations focus on truths Christians across churches share: God made you, God loves you, Jesus died and rose again, and God is with you.

Isn’t “declaring” a bit intense for children?

Not the way we’ve written it. For kids, a declaration is simply saying out loud what God says is true: “Jesus loves me.” “God gives me courage.” It’s like a memory verse in their own words, about their own life.

Does this replace Bible reading or church?

No. It’s a daily on-ramp: a short, joyful moment with God’s Word and a ready-made question to talk about. Families use it alongside Bible time, Sunday school and bedtime prayers.
